A business development tool for Golar LNG

Where should Golar's next floating LNG plant go?

Golar LNG turns natural gas into LNG on floating plants and leases them out on 20 year contracts. Its fourth unit is ordered for the end of 2029 and has no customer yet. This site puts live prices, satellite data and a global project database in one place to help answer where it should go.

    Start with the market

    A few words used throughout. LNG is natural gas cooled to a liquid so it can be shipped. FLNG is a floating plant that does the cooling at sea. MTPA is million tonnes of LNG a year; Golar's newest design makes 3.5. MMscf/d is million cubic feet of gas a day; a 3.5 MTPA plant needs about 500.

    01 · Market

    What today's prices mean for Golar

    Golar is paid a fixed fee for each unit. On its two Argentine units it also keeps 25% of the LNG price above $8/MMBtu. When European gas is expensive, that share is worth a lot.

        03 · Pitch

        What a Golar unit is worth to a gas owner

        The argument Golar makes to a customer: you keep your gas and most of the revenue, Golar builds and runs the plant for a yearly fee. Pick a field or a flaring lead from the map, and see who earns what at today's prices.

        Fee and price share follow the terms of Golar's Esperanza contract, scaled by capacity. 1 MTPA is about 52 million MMBtu a year. Feed gas cost includes 10% burned as fuel. IRR is unlevered over a 20 year charter with $2.45bn of capex per 3.5 MTPA and assumes today's price holds for all 20 years, so treat it as a ceiling when prices are high.
